Bitbox Micro Firmware: The Character Chain

Four Airwindows effect slots, sixteen effects to choose from.

Four effect slots in a row across the whole mix: Destruction, Overdrive, Colour and Saturation. Each slot holds one of four effects, sixteen in all. Every one is a port of a plugin by Chris Johnson of Airwindows, used under its MIT licence.

Turn a slot's Amount up and that effect joins the mix. At zero it is gone.

1The Four Banks

Each bank is a family of four. Every effect keeps its Airwindows name, so the Airwindows site has far more on each one.

Destruction

Destruction is lo-fi and breakage, for when the mix should sound broken on purpose: old samplers, aliasing, clang and buzz.

# Effect Sound Tone
1 Pockey2 old-sampler crunch and grit how far the sample rate drops
2 DeRez3 smeared, aliased lo-fi how few bits: harsher as you turn it up
3 Ring Mod metallic, bell-like clang the ring's pitch; fully down silences it
4 Fracture2 folds the wave into buzzy harmonics where the folding starts

Overdrive

Drive the whole mix, from a warm valve glow to big, dirty harmonics.

# Effect Sound Tone
1 Tube2 warm valve push how hard it is driven
2 Drive straight overdrive cuts the lows before the drive; near the top it thins and quietens
3 Mackity a small desk's input overloaded how much of the extra level is taken back
4 Huge big, loud, dirty harmonics Nasty: how harsh it gets

Colour

Colour is the sound of the gear rather than an effect: consoles, tape and a transformer. It is subtle at low Amounts, and a good first stop.

# Effect Sound Tone
1 Channel9 console warmth and edge which console: Neve, API, SSL, Teac or Mackie
2 IronOxide5 tape, with flutter and hiss tape speed: slower is darker
3 ToTape8 a fuller tape machine, with bump and flutter darker or brighter
4 Coils2 a transformer pushed hard; eases off at the very top from a good transformer to a cheap one

Saturation

Saturation adds weight and density, smoothly. It is the gentlest bank and comes last, so it rounds off everything before it.

# Effect Sound Tone
1 PurestDrive the cleanest, most transparent saturation how hard it drives, with the level held steady
2 Spiral2 smooth, rounded warmth presence
3 Density3 thick, dense saturation a low cut; its centre already thins the bass, so turn it down for full lows
4 Mojo fat saturation that lifts the level how much of the lift is taken back

2Where It Sits

The chain comes first on the master bus, before the EQ and the compressor, so they hear what it made. It hears every pad on Out 1 and 2, tails included. Pads on Out 3–6 skip it. See devices.

The slot order is fixed. Destruction comes first, then Overdrive, Colour and Saturation. You break things before you polish them.

3The Controls

Each slot has an Amount, a Tone and a way to pick its effect. They are all MIDI CCs on channel 1, like the EQ and the compressor.

Slot Amount Tone Next effect Pick effect
Destruction CC 14 CC 18 CC 26 CC 22
Overdrive CC 15 CC 19 CC 27 CC 23
Colour CC 16 CC 20 CC 28 CC 24
Saturation CC 17 CC 21 CC 29 CC 25

Amount is how much of the effect you hear. Zero is off. An off slot costs nothing.

Tone is each effect's most useful second control. The bank tables above say what it does. It starts at its centre.

To choose the effect, map one of the last two columns. You only need one.

  • Next effect is for a button. Each press steps to the slot's next effect and wraps back round after the fourth.
  • Pick effect is for jumping straight to one. Its range splits into four: 0–31 is the first effect, 32–63 the second, 64–95 the third and 96–127 the fourth. Use it from a sequencer, a DAW or a controller snapshot, when a song should always recall the same effect.

A change of effect fades out and back in. It never clicks.

Every move shows in the pop-up, such as OVERDRIVE Huge over Amount 76%.

4Things to Know

Three things are worth knowing before you stack slots.

CPU

One or two slots are light. All four at full, with the heaviest effect in each bank, take about a quarter of the processor. The two tape effects in Colour are the heaviest. To watch the load, turn on the CPU bar.

Quiet Tails Come Up

Saturation lifts quiet sound more than loud. Stack two or three slots and a reverb tail that used to fade away hangs on, with noise you never heard before. High makeup on the compressor lifts it again, and so does auto makeup at extreme settings. If tails turn long and grainy, back a slot off first.

Some effects change the level as well as the sound. Match the levels first. Then judge.

Not Saved

Like the other devices, the chain survives a preset change but not power-off. See what they have in common. At power-on every slot returns to Amount zero, its first effect and a centred Tone.
